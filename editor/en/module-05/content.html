SnakeBite Scenario: Business Case

Why do we use the Business Case practice?

Purpose of the practice:establish mechanisms to judge whether the project is and remains desirable, viable and achievable, supporting decisions on continued investment. This assessment supports decisions about further investment in the project.

It is an ongoing basis for decisions, not a document written once for budget approval. If business justification is lost, the project should be changed, recovered or stopped.

Justification testReview questionSnakeBite
Desirable - desirableDoes the balance of benefits, costs and risks justify the investment?Does the expected value of the game justify the investment and market risk?
ViableCan we supply the required products?Can the team and suppliers deliver the game and infrastructure under the agreed conditions?
AchievableIs use of the products likely to produce the intended outcomes and benefits?Even a finished game will not create the expected outcomes if it fails to reach players or they do not use it.

Exam trap:the feasibility of a product does not prove the feasibility of obtaining benefits. A project may be technically feasible and yet not make business sense.

On what basis do we document project justification?

Business case as a documentcompares estimated costs with expected benefits, taking into account the associated risks. It also determines how and when the benefits can be measured. The value of a project is more than the lowest cost or revenue alone.

ComponentWhat do we agree on?
Business reasons and goalsWhy is change needed and how will it contribute to the organization's strategy?
Business optionsWe compare: change nothing, do the minimum and do more than the minimum. No change is the baseline. A mandatory project also requires consideration of implementation variants.
Benefits and disadvantagesWe define measurable improvement and deterioration relative to the baseline situation, measurement deadlines and benefit tolerances.
Costs and financingWe take into account the implementation of the project, subsequent operation and maintenance, and sources of financing.
TimeWe separate the project duration from the period of obtaining benefits.
Investment and risk assessmentWe compare the value of benefits and disadvantages with inputs and additional operating costs. We disclose assumptions, key threats and opportunities, and responses to them.
SustainabilityWe set specific goals and tolerances related to the organization's goals.

Four activities that must be distinguished

  • Develop:Analyze your options and gather the information you need to evaluate your investment.
  • Check - check:assess whether the project is still worth implementing.
  • Keep up to date - maintain:update the rationale with performance data and the latest forecasts; keep previous versions for comparison.
  • Confirm - confirm:check the achievement or prospect of achieving benefits. Benefits may emerge during the project, although many will only occur after closure.

Outputs, capability, outcomes and benefits — the complete chain

In this modulean output is a specialist product, whereas an outcome is the result of change. The colloquial use of the word "result" in both senses blurs a key difference.

ConceptMeaningSnakeBite example
OutputA tangible or intangible product of work; in PRINCE2, a specialist product enabling change.Tested game, payment system, analytics and user manuals.
CapabilityA complete set of products needed to achieve the result.PixelBite has a complete solution that allows you to share the game, serve players and settle payments.
OutcomeA change in behavior or actual conditions resulting from implementing a change.Players regularly use the game and make purchases, and the company supports this new sales channel.
BenefitMeasurable improvement resulting from a result that is considered beneficial by the organization and supports its goals.Margin increase by a fixed amount relative to the no-project scenario, measured over a fixed period.
Dis-benefitMeasurable deterioration resulting from a result, assessed negatively and hindering the achievement of business goals.Confirmed increase in average waiting time for customers of other games when the shared support department also supports SnakeBite.
Business objectiveA measurable effect showing the progress of strategy implementation.Increasing the profitability of the game portfolio; SnakeBite benefits are designed to make a measurable contribution to this goal.

Relationship: outputs create the capability for change. Embedding and using that capability leads to outcomes. Outcomes can produce benefits and dis-benefits, and benefits contribute to business objectives. The relationship need not be one-to-one: several products can work together to enable one outcome.

There is no automatism:the reception of a game does not mean that players will accept it. Promotion, changing the way of working, support training and removing barriers to use may be needed. Disadvantage is not the same as uncertain risk - we assess the uncertainty of a negative event as a threat.

An approach to sustainable development management

Sustainability management approachis part of the Project Initiation Documentation (PID / PID). Identifies the actions, reviews and controls needed to achieve the project's sustainability goals.

Division of management-product roles: the Business Case defines sustainability objectives and tolerances and their relevance to business justification. The approach describes how they will be managed and achievement checked. The targets should support the organization's environmental, social and governance objectives.

Do not confuse the approaches: the benefits management approach organizes actions leading to outcomes and benefits reviews; the sustainability management approach organizes actions, measurement and control of sustainability targets. They may use some of the same data but have distinct purposes.

How to read the diagram: outputs are not benefits. A completed set of outputs creates capability; embedding it produces outcomes, measured through benefits and dis-benefits and connected to business objectives. The numeric values are teaching examples for SnakeBite, not textbook requirements.

Business justification is not only about cost. It concerns project value: the perceived benefits or importance of outcomes in proportion to resources used, and selection of the approach offering the best overall value.

Guidance for effective business case management

1. Business case lifecycleThe outline business case is prepared in SU, the full business case in IP, and it is then checked and maintained during stages, boundaries, exceptions, closure and benefits reviews.
2. Align products with objectives and tolerancesEach significant product should connect through capability and outcome to measurable benefit or controlled dis-benefit. Set benefit and sustainability tolerances alongside cost, time, risk, quality and scope limits.
3. Establish business justificationCompare options, costs, benefits, dis-benefits, risks and timing. Confirm desirability, viability and achievability and select the solution offering the best overall value.

1. Business case lifecycle — from mandate to full justification

The business case does not appear in its final form at the start. Its detail increases as knowledge of products, costs, timescales, risks and performance targets improves.

The outline business case is developed during Starting up a Project. It contains enough information for the Project Board to confirm initial business justification and authorize the Project Manager to initiate the project. It is part of the Project Brief, not a separate management product.

During Initiating a Project, more detailed plans and analysis refine it into the full business case. The Project Board reviews this when authorizing the project. The Business Case may be refined further as actual costs are incurred, forecasts mature and early benefits emerge.

2. Aligning products with business objectives and tolerances

The theory of outputs → capability → outcomes → benefits → business objectives is straightforward. Practice is not. A project can finish on time and within budget yet fail to produce the intended outcomes or benefits. It has then delivered products efficiently without creating the expected value.

The common failure: project teams focus on outputs because they are visible and produced before closure. Outcomes and benefits are often realized only afterwards. If the connection is not explicit, the original purpose can be lost and benefits remain only a forecast.

Not every product generates a benefit directly. Some products are enablers that allow other products to generate benefits. For example, support guidance does not create revenue itself, but without it operational capability may fail and players may not remain engaged.

ToleranceOfficial meaningWhere documented?
Benefits toleranceThe permissible deviation in benefit performance targets before escalation to the next management level is required.Business Case
Sustainability toleranceThe permissible deviation in sustainability performance targets before escalation to the next management level is required.Business Case

Do not confuse: tolerance is delegated freedom, not a target or a reserve to consume. A forecast breach triggers escalation under manage by exception.

3. Establishing business justification

The Business Case assessment considers all seven performance targets and their relationships. Trade-offs are common: a shorter timescale may increase cost or risk; more scope may reduce quality; and the cheapest option may weaken sustainability or benefits.

BenefitsCostTimeQualityScopeSustainabilityRisk

The Business Case considers business options for achieving the required outcomes and selects the option providing the best balance across all aspects. If no Business Case can remain within tolerances for all seven, the project's justification is questionable.

Do nothing differentlyThe baseline: what happens if the current way of operating continues?
Do the minimumThe smallest change capable of delivering part of the required outcomes.
Do more than the minimumA larger investment in exchange for additional scope, outcomes or benefits.

The difference between the options shows what the investment buys. Even a “must do” regulatory project should compare delivery options: the required outcome may be mandatory, but a single implementation option is not.

Business case management technique

1. DevelopGather evidence, analyse options and create justification appropriate to the current level of knowledge.
2. CheckAt decision points, assess desirability, viability and achievability using current forecasts.
3. MaintainUpdate costs, timescales, risks, outcomes and benefits, retaining a controlled history of change.
4. ConfirmVerify actual or forecast outcomes and benefits, including after project closure.

PRINCE2 technique: develop, check, maintain, confirm — in detail

1. Develop

Explore options and obtain the right information for investment appraisal decisions. The Project Mandate triggers SU and leads to the outline business case in the Project Brief. The Project Board approves the Project Brief when authorizing initiation. During IP, the outline business case is refined into the full business case, approved when authorizing the project.

Assess each option for desirability, viability and achievability and against all seven performance targets. Consider a broad range early, then reduce the options for detailed examination.

2. Check

At a minimum, the Project Board checks the Business Case:

  • at the end of SU, before authorizing initiation;
  • at the end of IP, before authorizing the project;
  • at the end of every stage, before authorizing the next stage and continued investment;
  • when assessing an Exception Plan, before authorizing the revised stage and continuation.

The Project Manager also checks it: when assessing progress, risks and issues; during the final stage as part of closing; and when stakeholder consultation suggests that goals have changed, such as new sustainability objectives.

3. Maintain

At the end of each stage, the Project Manager updates the Business Case with performance data: products delivered, actual project costs, benefits realized, and the latest forecast benefits and performance targets. The Business Case must reflect the current investment picture, not assumptions frozen at project start.

4. Confirm

The business layer reviews the Business Case through post-project benefits reviews to determine whether outcomes have realized benefits. Some benefits can be confirmed during the project, particularly with iterative releases, but much measurement will occur after closure.

Accountability for benefits: the Senior User specifies benefits and is accountable for confirming their realization, often beyond the project. The Executive ensures benefits reviews are planned and executed. At closure, responsibility for post-project measurement transfers from the Executive to the business layer — specifically the Senior User — together with funding and resources.

Benefits management approach

It defines the management actions required to achieve outcomes and confirm benefits. The Project Manager first creates it during IP in the initiation stage and submits it to the Project Board for approval when seeking project authorization. It may be managed by the project or by the business and is likely to continue beyond project closure. Benefits measurable during the project are confirmed by the Senior User for formal reporting by the Project Manager in End Stage Reports and the End Project Report.

Supporting Business Case techniques

Investment appraisal

Compares the costs of developing, operating and maintaining the project product with the value of benefits over time. It covers specialist delivery, project management, transition into BAU, and ongoing operations and maintenance.

TechniqueWhat does it show?
Whole-life costsTotal implementation, transition, operating and maintenance costs.
Net benefitsTotal benefit value minus implementation, transition and ongoing operation costs.
Return on investment — ROIProfit or savings expressed as a percentage of the initial investment.
PaybackThe time required to recover the invested cash and other resources.
Net present value — NPVToday's value of future cash flows after allowing for the time value of money and a discount rate.
Internal rate of return — IRRThe rate of return at which the investment's NPV equals zero.
Options analysisCompares options against weighted criteria to identify a shortlist and preferred option.
Sensitivity analysisVaries input factors to find the point at which the investment is no longer justified.

Multi-case model

Strategic perspectiveWhy is change needed and how does the investment fit strategy?
Economic perspectiveWhich option offers best value, including social, environmental and sustainability considerations?
Financial perspectiveIs the investment affordable; how will funding, budget and cash flow work?
Implementation and commercial perspectiveCan providers deliver the preferred option and are robust commercial and delivery arrangements in place?

A robust investment should satisfy all perspectives. The organization may use its own set of cases, such as a separate environmental case.

Best, expected and worst-case scenarios for benefits

With iterative-incremental delivery, scope is usually flexible while time and budget are more fixed. Benefits should therefore be presented as a range related to the amount of scope delivered, rather than one falsely precise estimate.

Best caseMust-, should- and could-have requirements are delivered; the highest realistic benefit forecast.
Expected caseMust-have and should-have requirements are delivered; the most likely forecast.
Worst caseOnly must-have requirements are delivered; even this case should show that the project remains viable.

Applying the practice in project context

🏢 OrganizationalAlign with strategy, portfolio priorities, investment policies, approval thresholds and how BAU realizes benefits.
🤝 CommercialCustomer and supplier may have different justifications. Contracts affect costs, risks, funding, benefit ownership and exit options.
🔄 Delivery methodFor iterative-incremental delivery, update forecasts as learning grows; funding and scope decisions may be incremental.
🌍 SustainabilityInclude targets, whole-life costs, resilience, project-work and product impacts, and sustainability tolerances.
↔️ ScaleMake analysis proportionate to value, risk, complexity and importance, from concise justification to a full investment model.
