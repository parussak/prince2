Chapter 10: Issues

Purpose and place in PRINCE2

The issues practice helps the project manager record, assess and control matters that need a decision. These include problems or concerns, external events, business opportunities, requests for change and off specifications. Change control protects approved product baselines. Assess effects on benefits, cost, time, quality, scope, risk and sustainability; seek the decision from the appropriate delegated authority.

SnakeBite: a decision and its consequence

Maya proposes a two player mode after the scope is approved. Tomek discovers that the ranking API misses its agreed response time. Zosia logs the first as a request for change and the second as an off specification. She assesses their impact and recommends action in an issue report. Tomek waits for an authorized decision before building the extra feature. Available change budget alone does not approve the work.

Exam terms and management products

Request for change: proposal to change an approved baseline. Off specification: something agreed is not, or is forecast not to be, delivered. Risk: an uncertain future event; an issue needs attention now. Concession: the Project Board’s acceptance of an off specification without corrective action.

The issue register tracks formal issues and decisions; an issue report records detailed analysis and a recommendation; the product register tracks versions and status. The change budget funds approved changes under agreed rules. An agile backlog does not confer change authority.

Issue types and the change decision

Problem or concern: a matter requiring attention, such as a team conflict or a defect affecting delivery. External event: an event outside the project requiring a response. Business opportunity: a possible change that could add value. Request for change: a proposal to alter an approved baseline. Off specification: something agreed has not been or will not be delivered as specified. A possible future supplier outage belongs in the risk register until it occurs or creates an issue that must be addressed.

The issue management approach defines how matters are recorded, analyzed and decided. For a material change, Zosia checks the effect on benefits, cost, time, quality, scope, risk and sustainability; she also checks any funding in the change budget and the limits of delegated change authority. The executive or board may need to decide if the effect crosses a delegated limit or threatens the business case. When a forecast exceeds the stage tolerance, the project manager escalates using an exception report.

The project log can include an issue register and daily log. The issue register tracks formal issues and their status. An issue report contains detailed assessment and options for significant matters. The product register shows the current status and approvals of affected products; product descriptions establish their agreed criteria. A risk register is for uncertain events and is not interchangeable with the issue register. Decisions and baseline updates are recorded so that implementers work against the correct approved version.

Additional textbook-aligned explanation

Issue categories and change control

The issues practice considers problems or concerns, business opportunities, external events, requests for change and off specifications. A request for change proposes an alteration to an approved baseline. An off specification is something agreed that is not delivered or is forecast not to be delivered. A concession is the Project Board’s acceptance of an off specification without corrective action. A risk is an uncertain future event; a current issue needs a decision now.

Assess, authorize and track

Capture the issue, assess its impact, recommend a response and seek a decision at the proper level of delegated authority. An issue register tracks formal issues and their status; an issue report details analysis and a recommendation. A daily log can hold informal items. The product register helps show which approved products and statuses are affected. Assess the seven performance targets: time, cost, quality, scope, benefits, risk and sustainability. The change budget may fund approved changes, but its availability is not approval. If the forecast exceeds delegated tolerance, escalate the exception.
