Chapter 2: Principles

Primary source: PRINCE2 7, Chapter 2.

What are the principles?

Principles are the guiding obligations that determine whether a project is genuinely using PRINCE2 as its management method. They support effective application and tailoring to any project.

PRINCE2 can be applied to virtually any project, environment and organization. The seven principles remain constant, while practices, processes, roles and management products are adapted to the context. Tailor the method; do not discard the principles.

🧭 The PRINCE2 authenticity test: a project must apply all seven principles. Merely using terms such as PID, business case or project board is not enough.

SnakeBite: seven decisions, one project

PixelBite Games aims to release the SnakeBite mobile game in six months. Artur represents the business, Maya the users, Kamil the suppliers, and Zosia manages the project. Then a competitor announces a similar game, the external API is delayed, and tests reveal excessive energy use.

The team does not merely tick off principles. It reassesses the investment, applies lessons from Pixel Knight, agrees roles and supplier relationships, divides the project into stages, sets tolerances, specifies products and quality, and tailors control to a small studio using iterative delivery.

1. ⚖️ Ensure continued business justification

Key message: a PRINCE2 project has business justification sufficient to warrant investment to initiate it and ongoing investment through to successful completion. If it does not, it should be stopped.

Throughout the life cycle, verify that the expected financial, strategic, social or other outcome remains desirable, viable and achievable. Sunk cost is not a reason to continue. Large organizations should also check whether another project duplicates the same justification or benefits.

SnakeBite: after the competitor’s announcement, Artur refreshes the market forecast. If worthwhile benefits are no longer realistic, the board changes or prematurely closes the project.
PartyPossible justification
CustomerMarket share, revenue or social change.
SupplierMargin, a strong reference or experience with new technology.
Co-ownerIts own value; withdrawal must consider contracts and consequences for others.

Trap: the business case is not written once and forgotten. It is maintained and reviewed at key decisions.

2. 🔄 Learn from experience

Key message: the team actively seeks, records and implements improvements from relevant prior and current lessons, applies them in future projects and shares them with others.

Three learning moments
Before
Search earlier work; repeat effective practices, not old mistakes.
During
Capture observations and apply lessons immediately where possible.
At closure
Consolidate and pass lessons to future projects.
SnakeBite: Pixel Knight’s lesson about late payment testing leads to an early prototype. A lesson matters only when it changes action.

Trap: lessons learned are not merely an end meeting or a report filed away.

3. 👥 Define roles, responsibilities and relationships

Key message: the project has agreed roles and responsibilities in a structure engaging business, user and supplier interests. The project management team also initiates and builds internal and external stakeholder relationships.

The right people must know what is expected, what authority they have and what they may expect from others. A clear, coherent structure enables effective delivery against all seven performance targets.

SnakeBite: for the API delay, Kamil assesses feasibility, Maya user impact and Artur business consequences. Relationships are actively maintained, not only charted.

4. 🪜 Manage by stages

Key message: a PRINCE2 project is planned, monitored and controlled on a stage-by-stage basis.

Definition: stage
The section of a project that the project manager is managing on behalf of the project board at any one time.

This focus ensures that the project is properly initiated before work starts on delivery. Every PRINCE2 project has at least two stages: an initiation stage and one further stage. Many projects benefit from more stages because they increase control and adaptability.

Why manage by stages?

AdvantageMeaning
AdaptabilityThe project can respond to changes in its project or business context.
Review and decision pointsThe board periodically assesses viability rather than allowing uncontrolled continuation.
Decision before detailed workKey approval occurs before the cost of detailed preparation and implementation.
External influencesThe project can clarify the effect of a budget cycle, finalized legislation, a standard or other dependency.
Manage by exceptionThe board delegates day-to-day stage control to the project manager within agreed tolerances.

How are stage boundaries selected?

Consider minimizing risk exposure through the life cycle; project size and complexity; major investment, business and technical decisions; required control points; and sector or business policies and standards. Shorter stages offer more control but demand more senior-management effort; longer stages reduce that effort but increase the interval between decisions.

The board authorizes one stage at a time and delegates day-to-day control to the project manager. While the stage is forecast to remain within tolerance, the project manager may make adjustments. The next stage is authorized only when sufficient business justification and funds remain. If the business case is no longer valid, the board determines recovery or premature closure.

SnakeBite and sprints: sprints organize delivery work; stages organize management and decisions. Zosia may place three sprints in the prototype stage and five in the release stage. PRINCE2 may use sprints and user stories instead of some work packages and product descriptions, but the board still authorizes the stage, not every sprint.

Do not confuse: a management stage need not match a technical phase, sprint or release. One stage may contain many sprints, and boundaries are selected for decisions and control.

5. 🚦 Manage by exception

Key message: a PRINCE2 project establishes limits of delegated authority by defining tolerances for performance against its plans.

Tolerance
The permissible deviation above and below a plan target for benefits, cost, time, quality, scope, sustainability and risk without escalating to the next management level.

In plain language, the higher level tells the lower: “You may decide within these limits.” When a breach is forecast, it becomes an exception and is escalated for a decision.

Teaching simplification: amber helps learners notice a trend. PRINCE2 has two decision states: the forecast remains within tolerance, or it forecasts an exception that must be escalated.

Tolerance operates at project, stage and team levels: business layer → project board → project manager → team/work package. Senior management retains control without micromanagement.

SnakeBite: when Zosia forecasts the API delay beyond time tolerance, she alerts the board. Iterative delivery often fixes time and cost tightly while allowing more scope flexibility.

Trap: escalate a forecast tolerance breach, not every deviation and not only after the breach has occurred.

6. 📦 Focus on products

Key message: a PRINCE2 project focuses on the definition and delivery of products, particularly their user quality expectations and requirements.

Definition: product
An input or output, tangible or intangible, that can be described in advance, created and tested.
Four PRINCE2 product types
📋 Management products
Information supporting management, such as a business case or plan.
🛠️ Specialist products
What users need to realize the required benefits.
🎯 The project product
The total output described in the project product description.
🌐 External products
Produced outside project control but required by it, such as a new standard.

In the guidance, “products” normally means specialist products unless a distinction is needed. Project products means the specialist and management products created during the project.

Plan from product to work
1. Project product
What must exist?
2. Component products
What comprises it?
3. Quality
How will it be accepted?
4. Activities
What work is needed?

An output-oriented project agrees and defines the project product before undertaking the activities required to produce it. The agreed product set defines scope and provides the basis for planning and control.

What does the product focus achieve?

  • The project performs only work that directly contributes to agreed products.
  • It controls scope creep by assessing every change through its impact on products and the business case.
  • It reduces user dissatisfaction and acceptance disputes because outputs are agreed early.
  • It supports controlled pause, closure and later resumption after defined products are completed.

Product descriptions support effort estimates, resource requirements, dependencies and activity schedules. Product focus supports planning, responsibilities, progress reporting, quality, change control, acceptance and risk management.

SnakeBite: “program the game” is an activity. “An accepted mobile game with defined modes, performance, payments and quality criteria” is a specialist product. The business case and stage plan are management products, while publication of a new app-store requirement may be an external product.

7. 🧩 Tailor to suit the project

Key message: PRINCE2 is applied and tailored to suit the project environment, size, complexity, importance, delivery method, team capability and level of risk.

Tailoring ensures that the method fits the project and aligns with business processes such as human resources, finance and procurement. Controls—the frequency and formality of reports, reviews, decisions and assurance—must be appropriate to scale, complexity, importance, delivery method, capability and risk.

Information, but not necessarily documents. Decisions, but not necessarily meetings. Effective management needs reliable information and conscious decisions; it does not automatically require extensive paperwork or a calendar full of ceremonies.

Who decides how to tailor?

The project manager is responsible for preparing recommendations after consulting relevant lessons and standards. The project board is accountable and approves them at the end of the initiation stage and whenever a change to the management approaches is proposed. Tailoring requires proactive choices, not accidental omissions caused by lack of time.

Commercial example: customer + agile supplier

A project may need to align two quality systems while the supplier uses an iterative-incremental in-house framework. To avoid confusion, the project manager may propose:

  • mapping terminology between PRINCE2 and the supplier’s agile approach;
  • adding customer and supplier subject-matter experts to the agile team;
  • using a coach to help teams combine the two approaches;
  • daily stand-ups and visual information radiators;
  • clear procedures for sprints or work packages, product descriptions or user stories.
SnakeBite: the studio uses concise digital management products and iterative delivery, while the API supplier uses its own terms and tools. Zosia maps the terminology, agrees the information flow and combines some meetings, but retains business, user and supplier representation and business-case control.

Trap: “it is small, so we skip the business case, roles and tolerances” is not tailoring. Failure to tailor leads either to unquestioning bureaucracy or to “heroic” improvisation.
