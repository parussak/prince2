Chapter 1: Introduction to PRINCE2

Primary source: PRINCE2 7, Chapter 1 (sections 1.2–1.6).

PRINCE2 as a project management method

PRINCE2 is a project management method with five integrated elements: principles, people, practices, processes, and the project context. They work together; they are not independent parts from which to select only the convenient ones.

How do the elements work together? The principles provide obligations that guide project decisions. The practices help ensure the principles are applied consistently during the activities described by the processes. The way they are applied is tailored to the project context. People are central: those working on the project, those making decisions, and those affected by its results. Their needs, capabilities, motivations, and relationships shape how the project is managed.

What is a project?

In PRINCE2, a project is a temporary organization established to deliver one or more business products in line with an agreed business case. It is a means of introducing business change, not simply a list of tasks or another name for its scope. Scope describes what the project will deliver and is one of its performance targets.

The products and scope can initially be described at different levels of detail, depending on what is known and how much uncertainty remains. They may be elaborated using the project's agreed controls as information becomes available. A request to change an approved scope baseline is an issue to be assessed and decided at the appropriate level of authority. Likewise, dates, costs, and quality criteria cannot be changed informally: teams work within agreed tolerances; changes beyond delegated authority require authorization or escalation.

SnakeBite: a project and business as usual

Artur, the project executive, wants to launch a new game called SnakeBite. A temporary team is formed: Zosia, the project manager, coordinates delivery; Maya, the senior user, represents user interests; and Kamil, the senior supplier, represents the delivery side. The project has a defined beginning, a planned end, products, and an agreed business case. Its work introduces a change in the organization's operations.

After the game is handed over and the project is closed, running the service, supporting players, and measuring some of the benefits become part of business as usual (BAU) — now operating in a new form with the change in place. If Maya proposes a new game mode after the release scope has been approved, Zosia assesses a request for change; the proposal does not automatically alter the agreed products.

Five characteristics that distinguish a project from BAU

CharacteristicWhat it meansSnakeBite example
ChangeProjects are a means of introducing change.The business launches a game and a new way of supporting it.
TemporaryA project has a defined start and end; after the change is implemented, BAU continues in its new form.The project team finishes, while operations take over the game.
Cross-functionalDifferent skills, roles, and sometimes organizations collaborate; the change affects people beyond the team.Developers, designers, marketing, support, and an external API supplier work together.
UniqueEven a similar project has a different team, environment, timeframe, supplier, or product.The studio has built games before, but this release has different requirements and partners.
UncertaintyChange and uniqueness bring threats and opportunities beyond those normally encountered in BAU.It is not yet known whether the new API integration will meet the forecast.

Remember: BAU sustains current operations; a project introduces agreed change, after which BAU operates in its changed form.

What is project management?

Project management applies methods, tools, techniques, and competencies to enable a project to meet its objectives. It involves planning, delegating, monitoring, and controlling the work, as well as motivating the people involved. It reduces and manages ambiguity: who decides, what is to be delivered and when, what resources are needed, and how to respond to change. It is more than producing documents.

Seven project performance targets

PRINCE2 manages seven project performance targets. Their priorities and tolerances — permitted ranges of deviation — help define how much discretion each management level has. Sustainability covers more than environmental impact alone.

TargetKey question
BenefitsWhy are we investing, for whom, and what improvement is expected?
CostIs the project affordable, and what would underspend or overspend mean for the budget?
TimeWhen will the project, its stages, and its key products be completed?
QualityHow will we know that the delivered products are fit for purpose?
ScopeWhat is included in the agreed delivery, and what is excluded?
SustainabilityWhat targets apply to project work and to the products in operation, such as energy use or resilience?
RiskHow much uncertainty is the business prepared to accept?

Priorities and tolerances should be explicit: an urgent project might have a narrower time tolerance than cost tolerance. A forecast breach of delegated tolerance triggers management by exception at the appropriate level.

Project context: where and how the method is applied

The project context is one of PRINCE2's five integrated elements. It includes four common contextual areas and the scale of the project. These are not sequential stages or optional extras: they help determine how the principles, people, practices, and processes are applied and tailored for a specific initiative.

🏢 Organizational contextWho represents the business, users, and suppliers? Is the project standalone or part of a programme or portfolio? How do strategy, policies, culture, and organizational capability affect decisions?
🤝 Commercial contextWhat customer–supplier relationships, contracts, procurement arrangements, and duties apply? A project may involve multiple organizations, prime contractors, and subcontractors.
🧭 Delivery methodHow will specialist work be performed and products created: through a linear-sequential, iterative-incremental, or hybrid approach?
🌍 Sustainability contextWhich organizational objectives and commitments apply to the project work and to the products during their operational life?
↔ Project scale cuts across every contextual area. It determines the proportionate governance, roles, documentation, controls, number of stages and work packages, and tolerances.

Delivery methods

A delivery method defines how the specialist work of the project will be performed. PRINCE2 provides the management environment and does not mandate a single development model. One project may use more than one delivery method.

Sustainability is embedded throughout the method

Sustainability is not synonymous only with environmental protection. It may concern the impact of project work, whole-life product costs, operational resilience, social effects, or the very purpose of the project. PRINCE2 incorporates sustainability across the method:

Sustainability
Performance targetOne of seven targets, with an agreed priority and tolerance.
RolesResponsibilities for agreeing, protecting, and monitoring sustainability requirements.
DocumentationRequirements, tolerances, and forecasts captured in plans, reports, and product descriptions.
PracticesFor example, the business case assesses the impact on project justification and value.
ProcessesActivities confirm requirements and revisit them at management decisions.

Scale and proportionality

Scale is relative to the organization: a project perceived as small by a global enterprise may be a major undertaking for a small company. It is therefore often more useful to distinguish simple from complex projects than merely small from large ones. PRINCE2 can be simplified for simple projects and expanded for complex projects.

Project scale is not the same as the technical scalability of a product. System scalability may be an important product requirement and may increase the project's complexity, risk, or importance, but in PRINCE2, “scale” describes how the organization perceives the project. It affects governance arrangements, the combining or separating of roles, the detail of management products, the formality of controls and reports, the selection of management approaches, the number of stages and work packages, and the tolerances set.

For SnakeBite, this means handling the external API provider within the commercial context, developing the game iteratively and incrementally, defining requirements for resource use and accessibility, and applying controls proportionate to the investment's risk and importance.

Features and benefits of PRINCE2

The benefits do not come from merely using document names. They arise when the method is applied as an integrated system of decisions, accountabilities, and controls:

ProvenBased on established and proven project management practice and governance used across many organizations.
UniversalCan be applied to any type of project and any scale, alongside specialist industry models and development lifecycles.
FlexibleCan be tailored to the specific needs of the organizations, the project, and its context without abandoning the principles.
Common language and conceptsProvides recognized vocabulary for all parties, improves consistency, supports handovers, and reduces the impact of personnel changes.
Outcome-focusedKeeps attention on project viability and business case objectives instead of treating project completion or task completion as an end in itself.
Increased organizational maturityPromotes learning from project experience and continual improvement in the organization.
Part of an integrated suite of methodsWorks with PeopleCert guidance for programme management, portfolio management, and risk management.

Practical effect for SnakeBite: the board, team, suppliers, and users share the same concepts; decisions remain connected to the intended outcome and business case; and lessons from the project can improve PixelBite's capability to run future initiatives.
